If your ability to provide value is what makes you a desirable asset, then knowledge was only ever half the equation. Knowledge has to become Execution — you have to put your work out in the world, and discuss and interact with people.

This isn't for people who want to keep collecting information and calling it progress. It's for people ready to develop systems in a manner that makes both the aspects work together — and engineer their luck.

Knowledge

Your ability to provide value is what makes you a desirable asset. So the question that should sit underneath everything you build is simple: how can you help the other person?

Execution: Engineer Your Luck

Knowledge on its own stays invisible. Execution is where you put your work out in the world, discuss and interact with people, and engineer your luck:

  • Increase surface area.
  • Collision creates chances.
  • Specific reputation attracts specific opportunities.
  • Luck travels through people.

In practice, Execution is a dedicated practice of translating your work into articles, tweets, interaction. So allocate dedicated time and effort towards it.

Let natural curiosity lead you to ask questions. Open-mindedness plus a willingness to learn means that, by design, you have cultivated the habit of thinking and questioning.

Document Everything

Document each and every development, and make it a daily habit. Psychologically it helps you understand progression and contemplate growth. The smallest of actions over a larger time frame contribute to a magnificent outcome. Again, it might come across as mundane or boring, but this is what helps bring results.

The work needs to translate into articles, then you put your work out, and with iteration you will get better. It requires deliberate practice. Again, this isn't intuitive, and it will require sustained effort.

Every aspect boils down to the same factors:

"Ordinary habits done over an extraordinary number of times."

Winning is simple — it's repeating the same habits again and again, and actively inculcating feedback.

Pain Brings Growth

Constantly work on the areas that you have to improve. Deliberately work on your weaknesses. Put yourself through the pain. Your brain will naturally tend to avoid this pain — why? Because by design it is meant to prioritize your survival; anything that threatens it will be avoided.

That's exactly why you need to master the habit of constantly putting yourself through the pain. Pain brings growth.

Example — Muscle Development: Muscle growth happens when you lift weights slightly higher than what your body is accustomed to, and over repeated efforts you become comfortable with it and are required to repeat the same process all over again by increasing the weights. In the present you have to endure pain and discomfort; this by itself is an indicator of growth, even though it is unintuitive and against human nature.

How to Work Against the Natural Tendency to Avoid Pain

  1. Normalize that the habits aren't threatening.
  2. Make the behaviour / habit rewarding — through consistent rewards and variable rewards.
  3. Find like-minded individuals to normalize the behaviour.

Think in Systems

Constantly putting yourself through that pain requires a lot of cognitive energy or will power. It is difficult in nature, as it's against human nature and it's threatening. So think in systems and develop routines. Reduce cognitive load, and make it easier for you to make those decisions.

Sit at the same time, in the same place, eat the same food. This normalizes behaviour and makes it less threatening. Rewarding it — consistently or variably — makes the routine repeatable, and with iteration you get comfortable.

Why the Loop Matters

The Execution Loop is important because it creates your social presence, building your digital identity and engineering your luck. As Naval Ravikant put it: luck travels through people.

So don't try to overhaul everything at once. Just allocate dedicated time and effort, document each and every development, and make it a daily habit.
